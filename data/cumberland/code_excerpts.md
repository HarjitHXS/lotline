# Cumberland, RI: verbatim code excerpts used to ground the chat
Read 2026-10-16 from eCode360 (https://ecode360.com/30187156 = Appendix B Zoning; https://ecode360.com/30186134 = Appendix A Land Development Regulations) and RIGL (webserver.rilegislature.gov). The posted code may lag recent amendments (eCode360 lists Ord. 26-17A and 26-22 as new).

## Appendix B § 6-6 Accessory living units
(a) One ADU per lot is permitted by right under the following circumstances: (1) On an owner-occupied property as a reasonable accommodation for family members with disabilities; or (2) On a lot with a total lot area of 20,000 square feet or more for which the primary use is residential; or (3) Where the proposed ADU is located within the existing footprint of the primary structure or existing accessory attached or detached structure and does not expand the footprint of the structure.
(b) An ADU shall be on the same lot where the principal use is a single-family or multifamily dwelling unit; (c) The ADU shall provide complete independent living facilities for one or more persons; (d) The ADU may include up to two bedrooms; (e) The minimum number of off-street parking spaces required for an ADU is one per bedroom; (f) The ADU shall comply with all state agency regulations and building code requirements; (g) The size of the ADU may be no more than 60% of the floor area of the principal dwelling or 900 square feet of floor area for a studio or one-bedroom ADU and 1,200 square feet of floor area for a two-bedroom ADU, whichever is less; (h) The ADU shall meet all the dimensional requirements of Article 5 of this appendix; (i) Zoning relief shall not be required for ADU applications proposed within an existing footprint of the primary or accessory structure which is a legal nonconforming structure in order to address the existing dimensional nonconformity, however, a proposed ADU shall not be prohibited for failure to meet existing dimensional nonconformities in a legally nonconforming structure; (j) The ADU shall not be offered or rented for tourist or transient use or through a hosting platform.

## Appendix B § 6-2 Number of buildings on a lot
There shall not be more than one main residential building and up to two accessory buildings on one lot, except in A and I zones where there may be more than two accessory buildings. Except as otherwise allowed as part of a multifamily development, there shall not be more than one main or principal residential building on a single lot used for residential purposes. A Special Use Permit may be granted under Unified Development Review when the application meets the criteria in § 18-3(w), which allows the subdivision of a single parcel containing multiple pre-existing, legally non-conforming dwellings so that each dwelling is on a single lot.

## Appendix B § 5-2 Dimensional regulations, agricultural and residential zones (summary of the three tables)
Which table applies depends on utilities: (a) neither public water nor sewer, (b) water or sewer but not both, (c) both. Minimum lot area per dwelling unit with both water and sewer: R-2 is 10,000 sq ft plus 5,000 for the 2nd unit; R-3 is 10,000 sq ft plus 5,000 for each additional unit; R-1 25,000; A-2 80,000; A-1 217,800. R-2 and R-3 setbacks (water and sewer): front 10 ft, side 10 ft, rear 15 ft, maximum lot coverage 40%, maximum height 35 ft, minimum frontage 40 ft. Where an area is principally built, a reduction in the minimum front yard is allowed under § 5-4.

## Appendix B § 18-4 Modifications
(a) In applications for relief from the literal dimensional requirements of this appendix as contained in Article 5 involving the construction, alteration, or structural modification of a structure or lot of record, the Zoning Officer shall be authorized to grant approval in the form of a modification permit. Such modification shall not exceed 15% of any dimensional requirement specified in this appendix.
(b) Within 10 days of receipt of a request for a modification, the Zoning Officer shall make a decision as to the suitability of the requested modification, based on: (1) The modification requested is reasonably necessary for the full enjoyment of the permitted use; (2) If the modification is granted, neighboring property will neither be substantially injured nor its appropriate uses substantially impaired; (3) The modification requested does not violate any rules or regulations with respect to freshwater wetlands; (4) The modification requested does not require a variance of a flood hazard requirement, unless the building is built in accordance with applicable regulations.
(c) In the case of a modification of 5% or less, the Zoning Officer shall have the authority to issue a permit approving the modification without any public notice requirements.
(d) In the case of a modification greater than 5%, the Zoning Officer shall notify, by first class mail, all property owners abutting the property, and shall publish notice in a newspaper of local circulation that the modification will be granted unless written objection is received within 14 days. If a written objection is received within 14 days, the request shall be scheduled for the next available hearing before the Zoning Board of Review on application for a dimensional variance, following the standard procedures for such variances. If no written objection is received within 14 days, the Zoning Officer shall grant the modification.

## Appendix B § 17-8 Powers of the Zoning Board of Review
The board hears appeals from decisions of administrative officers, hears and decides applications for variances (Article 18) and special use permits (Article 18), and can issue conditional zoning approvals where state or federal approvals are pending. The Building Official serves as staff to the board. Decisions granting a variance or special use permit are recorded in the land evidence records.

## Appendix A § 2 Definitions (Land Development and Subdivision Regulations)
MINOR LAND DEVELOPMENT PROJECT: a land development project involving any one of: (1) 7,500 gross sq ft or less of new commercial, manufacturing or industrial floor area; (2) an expansion of up to 50% of existing floor area or up to 10,000 sq ft for commercial, manufacturing or industrial structures; (3) mixed-use development of up to six dwelling units and 2,500 or less gross sq ft of commercial space; (4) multifamily residential or residential condominium development of nine units or less; (5) change in use where no extensive construction is sought; (6) an adaptive reuse project of up to 25,000 gross sq ft in a commercial zone with no extensive exterior construction; (7) an adaptive reuse project in a residential zone which results in less than nine residential units.
MINOR SUBDIVISION: a subdivision creating nine or fewer buildable lots. MAJOR SUBDIVISION: 10 or more buildable lots. MAJOR LAND DEVELOPMENT: any land development project that exceeds the minor thresholds. NOTICE AREA: two hundred feet from the perimeter of the entire property.

## Appendix A § 5-E Pre-application meetings
Pre-application meetings are required for all major land development or subdivision applications, and may be held for administrative, minor, and development plan review applications on request of the Planning Board, Administrative Officer, or applicant. Pre-application discussions are for guidance and are not approval of a project.

## Appendix A § 5-G Minor land development and subdivision
(a) Review has two stages, preliminary and final; the Administrative Officer may combine them. (b) Applications needing only a modification: file the application and a modification request with the Zoning Enforcement Officer; if granted, the Administrative Officer reviews it; if denied or an objection is received, it goes to unified development review. Applications needing a variance or special use permit are reviewed by the Planning Board under unified development review, and the request for review accompanies the preliminary plan application. A street creation or extension is reviewed by the Planning Board with a public hearing. (d) Certification: the application is certified complete or incomplete by the Administrative Officer within 25 days (15 days if no street creation or extension) when a completed checklist is provided; the clock stops on a certificate of incompleteness and restarts on resubmission, but staff need not certify a corrected submission less than 10 days after resubmission. (g) Decision: without street creation or unified development review, the Administrative Officer decides the preliminary plan within 65 days of certification. With street creation or unified development review the Planning Board holds a public hearing and decides within 95 days of certification. (h) Failure to act within the period is approval of the preliminary plan. (j) Final plan: the Administrative Officer decides within 25 days of certification of completeness. (k) Approval of a minor plan expires one year from approval unless recorded or extended.

## Appendix A § 5-H Major land development and major subdivision
Three stages after the required pre-application meeting: master plan, preliminary plan, final plan. A public hearing is required (at master plan). The Administrative Officer certifies each stage complete or incomplete within 25 days.

## Appendix A § 5-I Development plan review
Administrative development plan review (Administrative Officer): a change in use where no extensive construction is sought. Formal development plan review (Planning Board, preliminary and final stages): adaptive reuse with no extensive exterior construction; development that clears, grades or disturbs more than 10,000 sq ft; a new parking area over 5,000 (amended text shows 2,000) sq ft; mixed-use development of up to six dwelling units or 2,500 or less gross sq ft of commercial space; commercial or industrial development using or storing hazardous materials.

## 2026 Planning Board / TRC schedule note
The Planning Board reviews major land development/subdivisions, Unified Development Review, Formal Development Plan Review and Comprehensive Permits. The Administrative Officer reviews minor land development/subdivisions without UDR and Administrative Development Plan Review. All projects are first reviewed by the Technical Review Committee. Land development or subdivision projects seeking a modification must first apply to the Zoning Officer. Zoning applications that do not need land development or subdivision review are due to the Zoning Clerk by the first Friday of the month to be heard by the Planning Board at its next meeting. A deadline does not guarantee a Planning Board slot: the application must first be certified complete and reviewed by the TRC.

## RIGL § 45-24-73 (state ADU law, overrides local rules)
One ADU per lot is allowed by right: (1) owner-occupied property as a reasonable accommodation for family members with disabilities; (2) lot of 20,000 sq ft or more with a primary residential use; (3) within the existing footprint of the primary structure or an existing accessory structure without expanding it. A town may cap ADU size, but the cap must allow at least 900 sq ft or 60% of the principal dwelling (whichever is less) for a studio or one-bedroom ADU, and 1,200 sq ft or 60% (whichever is less) for a two-bedroom ADU. A town may not restrict ADU tenants by family relationship or age unless a federal subsidy requires it.

## RIGL § 45-24-41 Variances (standards)
Evidence must show: (1) the hardship is due to the unique characteristics of the subject land or structure and not to the general characteristics of the surrounding area, and is not due to a physical or economic disability of the applicant; (2) the hardship is not the result of any prior action of the applicant; (3) the variance will not alter the general character of the surrounding area or impair the intent or purpose of the zoning ordinance or comprehensive plan. For a use variance, the land cannot yield any beneficial use if required to conform. For a dimensional variance, the hardship amounts to more than a mere inconvenience: the relief is minimal to a reasonable enjoyment of the permitted use; more profit is not grounds. Public hearing with at least 14 days' newspaper notice and first-class mail to abutters; the applicant pays for notice.

## Other state laws that may apply (from the Housing Jam Q1 pack, Technical Paper 173; confirm current text)
Adaptive reuse of commercial buildings to at least 50% residential is a permitted use (RIGL 45-24-31, 45-24-37). Substandard lots of record: an owner need not seek relief solely because the lot is below the minimum lot size (RIGL 45-24-38, amended 2023 to 2026). Oversized lot subdivisions (RIGL 45-23-32, 2026). Special use permits need objective criteria (RIGL 45-24-42). Zoning certificates must be issued within 20 days of a written request (RIGL 45-24-54). Townhouses are permitted in residential districts that allow two or more units (RIGL 45-24-33, effective Jan 1, 2026). Parking: a town may require no more than one off-street space per unit within 1/4 mile of a regional mobility hub or 1/8 mile of a frequent transit stop (RIGL 45-24-33, effective June 23, 2026).
